# "He Flew a Kite Into a Thunderstorm and Somehow Survived"
Topic: **Atmospheric Electricity & Lightning Rods** (Benjamin Franklin, 1752). Format: punchy documentary short (~64 s).

## Facts
- In 1752, Benjamin Franklin famously tested whether lightning is electrical using a silk kite, hemp string, a brass key, and a Leyden jar.
- Standing under a dry shed to stay insulated, he saw the loose hemp fibers stand up and felt a spark jump from the key to his knuckle.
- This led directly to his invention of the lightning rod, saving thousands of wooden buildings from fires.

## Script
Most people run indoors during a lightning storm—Benjamin Franklin ran outside with a kite and a metal key.
In 1752, scientists still argued that lightning was mysterious fire from the sky, completely different from lab electricity.
Franklin bet his life that a thundercloud was just a giant static battery waiting to be tapped.
So he built a kite out of silk, tied a brass key near the bottom, and waited for the darkest storm of the year.
Even his own son thought the experiment was a death wish as thunder shook the sky above Philadelphia.
Holding a dry silk ribbon inside a wooden shed so the current wouldn't fry him, Franklin watched the wet string.
Suddenly, the loose fibers on the string stood straight up like bristles on an angry cat.
He reached his knuckle toward the brass key—and a sharp blue spark snapped across the air!
Luckily, Franklin survived to publish his proof that lightning is pure electricity.
Unluckily, the next guy who tried to copy his experiment in Russia wasn't so careful and died instantly.
You see, storm clouds build up trillions of volts of static charge, desperate to find the shortest path to the ground.
It's just like shuffling your socks across a carpet and zapping a metal doorknob in the dark.
Franklin realized a tall pointed iron rod on a roof could calmly drain that charge straight into the dirt.
Within months, lightning rods were saving churches, ships, and homes from burning to ashes.
So every skyscraper antenna you see today is just Franklin's crazy storm kite wearing a suit of steel.
