# The Giant Pendulum That Proved Earth Spins Under Your Feet

## Script
In 1851, a quiet physicist named Leon Foucault invited Paris into a grand stone cathedral to watch a brass ball swing.
He hung a twenty-eight-kilogram sphere from a sixty-seven-meter steel wire right beneath the towering Pantheon dome.
At the bottom, a sharp metal stylus grazed a circular ring of damp sand surrounding the marble floor.
Foucault burned a cotton thread to release the weight smoothly without giving it any sideways push at all.
Back and forth the heavy pendulum sliced through the silent hall like a hypnotic bronze metronome.
Wait a few minutes, and the crowd gasped: the line scratched into the sand was slowly rotating clockwise.
Nobody touched the wire, yet the swing path kept twisting hour after hour around the compass rose.
Then tiny wooden pegs placed along the rim began toppling over one by one with sharp clicks.
The secret flipped common sense upside down: the pendulum wasn't twisting—the cathedral floor was turning underneath it.
Every city in France wasn't standing still; the whole planet was spinning beneath that stubborn swinging sphere.
Imagine drawing a straight chalk line across a spinning merry-go-round: the floor curves away beneath a straight path.
Because the heavy bob remembers its straight plane in space, Earth's rotation turns the walls around the swing.
The closer you stand to the North Pole, the faster the floor spins completely underneath in twenty-four hours.
Today, science museums around the world keep Foucault pendulums knocking down pegs as silent proof of our spinning globe.
So even when the ground feels rock solid beneath your shoes, remember you are riding a giant planetary carousel.

## Facts
- Leon Foucault's 1851 Pantheon pendulum demonstration in Paris using a 67 m wire and 28 kg bob (Wikipedia).
- Conservation of swing plane in an inertial frame makes the Earth appear to rotate beneath the pendulum at angular rate omega * sin(latitude) (physics calculation).
