# "Sixteen Horses Couldn't Pull Apart Empty Air" — original script v1 (2026-11-09)
Topic: **Atmospheric Pressure & Thermodynamics** (major physics topic). Format: cartoon-3d-short skill (~62 s, ~3.7 words/s), one steady voice, English only, no captions.

## Facts (each joke is a real fact)
- On 8 May 1654 in Regensburg, Otto von Guericke (Mayor of Magdeburg and inventor of the vacuum pump) demonstrated the Magdeburg hemispheres before Emperor Ferdinand III and the Imperial Diet. (Wikipedia, "Magdeburg hemispheres")
- Two copper hemispheres about 50 cm (20 in) in diameter were fitted together with a greased leather ring—no bolts, latches, or glue—and the air was pumped out. (same)
- Two teams of 8 horses (16 horses total) pulling in opposite directions could not separate the evacuated sphere. (same)
- As soon as Guericke opened the stopcock valve to let air back in, the hemispheres fell apart on their own. (same)
- At sea level, atmospheric pressure (~101.3 kPa) on a 50 cm circle produces ~20,000 N (~2 tonnes / 4,500 lbf) of inward force. (standard fluid mechanics calculation)

## Script
Sixteen warhorses tried to pull apart two copper bowls held together by nothing at all, and lost.
It's 1654 in Germany, and Mayor Otto von Guericke has invited the Emperor to watch a tug-of-war against empty space.
He presses two hollow copper bowls together into a ball, with no bolts, no latches, and not a drop of glue.
Then he hooks up a brass hand pump and sucks the air out from inside.
The crowd laughs when he hitches eight horses to the left side and eight horses to the right.
The whips crack, thirty-two hooves dig into the dirt, the ropes groan, and the copper ball doesn't budge.
Then Guericke walks up, turns one tiny brass valve with two fingers, and the bowls fall apart on their own.
Luckily, there was nothing inside the ball pulling the halves shut.
Unluckily, there was an entire ocean of air outside pushing them shut.
You see, a vacuum never sucks; the air around you pushes.
Ever pressed a rubber plunger flat against a smooth tile floor?
The empty cup inside isn't grabbing the floor; miles of heavy sky above your head are sitting on the rubber, pinning it down.
With no air left inside to push back, the sky was clamping those copper bowls together with two tonnes of force.
Guericke proved that invisible air has crushing weight, paving the way for the steam engine.
Which means sixteen warhorses were beaten in a tug-of-war by the weather.
