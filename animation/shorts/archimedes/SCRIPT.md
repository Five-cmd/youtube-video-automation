# "The Fake Gold Crown That Made a Genius Run Naked Down the Street"
Topic: **Buoyancy & Water Displacement** (Archimedes, ~250 BC). Format: punchy documentary short (~64 s).

## Facts
- King Hiero II of Syracuse suspected a goldsmith replaced some of the gold in his votive crown with cheaper silver.
- Archimedes realized that stepping into a full bathtub made water overflow equal to his body's volume.
- Since gold is denser than silver, a silver-mixed crown of the same weight takes up more volume and displaces more water.

## Script
A paranoid king handed a scientist a golden crown and made him an offer: prove it's fake, or lose your head.
Around 250 BC, King Hiero suspected his goldsmith had secretly mixed cheap silver into his royal crown.
The catch? Archimedes wasn't allowed to melt, scratch, or damage the crown in any way.
For days, he stared at the shiny crown on his table while the king's guards waited impatiently outside.
If he guessed wrong, he'd look like a fool. If he stayed silent, he was finished.
Exhausted, Archimedes climbed into a bathtub filled all the way to the rim.
The moment he sank in, water spilled right over the edge onto the tile floor—and his brain exploded.
He realized his body pushed out an exact volume of water, and jumped straight out screaming "Eureka!"
Luckily, he discovered the secret of density and displacement.
Unluckily, he forgot to put his clothes back on before running down the street.
You see, gold is much denser than silver, so a pound of silver takes up way more space than a pound of gold.
Drop both into separate jars of water, and the bulkier silver pushes the water level higher.
When Archimedes dunked the crown against pure gold of the exact same weight, the crown spilled more water.
The cheating goldsmith was busted without a single scratch on the royal gold.
And that's why even a giant steel cargo ship floats—as long as it pushes aside more water weight than it weighs itself.
