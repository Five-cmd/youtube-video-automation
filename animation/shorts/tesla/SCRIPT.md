# "The Giant Tower That Tried to Give the World Free Wireless Power"
Topic: **Wireless Power & High-Voltage Resonance** (Nikola Tesla, 1901). Format: punchy documentary short (~64 s).

## Facts
- In 1901, Nikola Tesla built the 187-foot Wardenclyffe Tower on Long Island, funded initially by J.P. Morgan.
- Tesla demonstrated wireless lighting of lamps using high-frequency alternating current and resonant coils.
- J.P. Morgan pulled funding when he realized wireless broadcast power couldn't easily be metered and sold.

## Script
In 1901, Nikola Tesla built a one-hundred-eighty-seven-foot tower designed to shoot free electricity through the air to the entire planet.
Tesla hated ugly copper power lines hanging over every street and believed the Earth itself could carry electrical waves.
He convinced billionaire J.P. Morgan to fund a massive mushroom-shaped tower on Long Island called Wardenclyffe.
Morgan thought he was paying for a transatlantic radio station to beat Marconi in the news business.
Instead, Tesla quietly engineered a giant resonant coil meant to light up lamps miles away without a single wire.
When locals walked near the field at night, blue lightning arcs crackled off the dome and illuminated the clouds.
Then Tesla allegedly confessed his real dream to Morgan: sending free wireless energy to everyone on Earth.
Morgan asked one cold question: "Where do I put the meter?"—and pulled every dollar of funding overnight.
Luckily, Tesla's alternating current still won the war and powers every outlet in your wall today.
Unluckily, his giant tower was blown up with dynamite in 1917 for scrap metal.
You see, a Tesla coil works like two tuning forks—when both vibrate at the exact same frequency, energy jumps across empty air.
Push one tuning fork with a gentle hum, and a matching fork across the room starts ringing by itself.
Tesla used tuned high-frequency coils to push power across rooms with zero cables attached.
Over a century later, that exact resonance trick is what charges your phone on a wireless pad tonight.
Tesla didn't lose because his physics failed—he lost because you can't put a price tag on free air.
