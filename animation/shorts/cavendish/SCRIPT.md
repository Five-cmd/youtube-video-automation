# "The Man Who Left the Room to Weigh the World": original script v1 (2026-11-09)
Topic: **Gravitation** (major physics topic). Format: cartoon-3d-short skill, ~62 s, ~3.7 words/s, one steady voice, English only, no captions.

## Facts (each joke is a real fact)
- Henry Cavendish ran the torsion-balance experiment in 1797-1798 in an outbuilding at his house in Clapham, London. (Wikipedia, "Cavendish experiment")
- The apparatus was designed by John Michell, who died in 1793 before using it; Cavendish rebuilt it. (same)
- A 6 ft (1.8 m) wooden rod hung from a wire, with a 1.61 lb (0.73 kg) lead ball on each end; two 348 lb (158 kg) lead balls were placed nearby. (same) -> each big ball is heavier than two adult men.
- The twisting force was about 1.74e-7 N, ~1/50,000,000 of the small balls' weight. (same)
- To avoid air currents and temperature changes, the apparatus was enclosed in a wooden box in a closed shed, and Cavendish watched the rod through telescopes from outside, reading a scale. (same)
- Cavendish was famously shy, the richest scientist of his era ("the richest of all learned men"); he communicated with his servants by notes. (Wikipedia, "Henry Cavendish")
- His result gave Earth's density 5.448 x water; this fixes Earth's mass (~5.97e24 kg = ~6 billion trillion tonnes). (same)
- G is the least precisely known fundamental constant (CODATA relative uncertainty ~2e-5, far worse than other constants). (Wikipedia, "Gravitational constant")
- A small fridge magnet holding paper up against the whole Earth's pull is the standard demonstration that gravity is the weakest force. (common physics teaching example)

## Script
The man who weighed the entire planet was too shy to stay in the room with his own experiment.
It's 1797, and Henry Cavendish, the richest and quietest scientist in London, has a strange wooden box in his garden shed.
Inside, a six-foot rod hangs from a thin wire, with a little lead ball on each end.
Beside them sit two giant lead balls, each heavier than two grown men.
The plan sounds ridiculous: wait for the big balls to pull the small ones, using nothing but gravity.
But the pull is so weak, even the warmth of his own body would wreck it.
So Cavendish locks the shed, walks outside, and spies on the rod through a telescope in the wall.
For hours, it creeps sideways by just a few millimetres.
Luckily, gravity pulls on absolutely everything.
Unluckily, gravity pulls on absolutely everything.
Every draft and every warm wall nudges that rod too.
You see, gravity is the weakest force you will ever meet.
Ever stuck a fridge magnet on a sheet of paper? That tiny magnet is beating the entire planet pulling the paper down.
So two lead balls tugging on each other is a whisper in a thunderstorm.
But Cavendish heard the whisper, and from it, the weight of the Earth fell out: about six billion trillion tonnes.
Two hundred years later, that gravity number is still the least precise constant in physics.
Maybe scientists should try leaving the room.
