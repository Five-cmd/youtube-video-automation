# The Train Band That Proved Light Waves Stretch

## Script
In 1845, a physicist named Christian Doppler hired an entire brass band and put them on a moving train.
His wild claim sounded crazy: motion itself squeezes and stretches waves of sound and light.
Skeptical scientists stood beside the railway tracks in Utrecht with tuning forks, convinced music stays the same pitch.
The steam locomotive roared toward the station carrying fifteen trumpeters blasting one steady musical note at full volume.
As the open railcar raced closer, the notes sounded noticeably sharper and higher than what the band actually played.
The instant the train thundered past the platform, the exact same note suddenly dropped flat and low.
Listeners on the wooden platform blinked in shock as the melody bent downward right before their ears.
Doppler explained that the moving train chased its own sound waves forward, cramming the crests closer together ahead of it.
Meanwhile, behind the speeding train, each wave stretched farther apart, lowering the pitch into a deep groan.
For two whole days, the band rode back and forth until every stubborn critic heard the shift with their own ears.
You see, waves behave like ripples on water when the source starts moving through the medium.
Every ambulance siren screaming high as it approaches and dropping low as it passes proves Doppler right every day.
Even better, astronomers soon realized the exact same stretching happens to starlight across the entire universe.
Distant galaxies rushing away stretch their light toward red, letting us measure that space itself is expanding.
So the next time a siren wails past your street, remember a nineteenth-century brass band on a runaway flatcar.
